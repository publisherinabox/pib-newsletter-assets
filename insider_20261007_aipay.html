 INSIDER ANALYSIS

Google is paying some publishers for AI answers

Invite-only, opaque, and for most of the ones talking, tiny. Here’s what the pilot actually is, and what we’d do if we’re not on the list.

For months, publishers have asked whether Google would ever pay for the content that feeds AI answers. A quiet Search Console pilot is the first real answer, and the leaked numbers make it a hard sell for anyone who still has licensing leverage.

01What the pilot is

Google is running an invite-only AI contribution pilot inside Search Console. Reporting from The Information, via Search Engine Journal and Ars Technica, puts the paid cohort at roughly 100 publishers.

The program pays when your content “significantly” contributes while an AI answer is being generated in the Gemini app, AI Overviews, or AI Mode. Being cited, linked, or used to confirm a fact after the answer is generated does not qualify. That distinction matters, and Google has not published a clean definition of “significantly.”

Participants see a monthly earnings widget in Search Console with some history, no formula, and a standing option to opt out. The pilot was first spotted around April 2026, Digiday covered the invitation wave on Sept 14, and The Information’s late-September reporting put hard numbers on the table.

02Who gets paid (and for what)

You only get in if Google invites you. Small and midsize publishers have been more willing to join. Larger outlets have been more likely to decline, hoping for better terms later and worrying that joining now weakens their hand in licensing talks.

What earns is contribution during generation, not the citation that shows up at the bottom of an answer. Participants also say niche topics with strong demand and thinner coverage, like anime and gaming, appear to pay better. That is still anecdote, not a published rate card.

03The numbers that leaked

The Information’s figures come from different publishers over different stretches of time. Taken together, they sketch a wide, uneven range:

~100

publishers being paid in the invite-only Search Console pilot.

<0.1%

of ad revenue for several small and midsize participants talking.

<$1k

over several months for some of the smaller sites in the cohort.

$1M+ / yr

reported by one early niche winner. A later entrant: about $50k to $60k so far.

Those are not averages. They are the leaked range. Month-to-month swings without explanation are a common complaint, which makes planning around the widget almost impossible.

04Why big publishers are sitting out

For a large brand, a lowball pilot rate is not just a weak check. It is a precedent. Digiday and The Information both report that bigger houses have declined, hoping Google and other AI platforms will eventually pay more for licensed content.

The fear is simple: join now, and you train the market that AI contribution is worth a fraction of a percent of ad revenue. That is a hard number to walk back later in a licensing negotiation.
